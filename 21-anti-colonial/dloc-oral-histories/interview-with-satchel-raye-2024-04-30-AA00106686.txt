Abstract:
Avi Ackerman conducted this interview with Satchel Raye as part of the Gainesville
Business History Project at the Inquire Capitalism Program with the cooperation of the
Samuel Proctor Oral History Project. Mr. Satchel Raye is the owner of Satchel’s Pizza
located on 1800 NE 23rd Ave, Gainesville, FL, which opened in 2003. Throughout the
interview Mr. Raye discusses the intersection of art, music and pizza. Serving as a
gathering place for the Gainesville community and a tourist destination, Satchel’s Pizza
has battled two separate fires and emerged from the flames stronger as a whole, both
times. Over the twenty plus years of operating, the restaurant has had various art, designs,
artifacts, tables and collections; notably a lint collection, pine cone contests, large rubber
band balls and more. The restaurant itself has lots of outdoor dining areas; the van in the
front and the covered greenhouse dining area situated under a suspended airplane. These
outdoor spaces made operating during the COVID-19 pandemic seamless. Mr. Raye
considers the staff one of the greatest successes of the restaurant. Employees are paid a
living wage with paid vacation and retirement accounts while also implementing a profit
sharing program for managers. Furthermore, Satchel’s has been a source of hope in the
community through their round-up and grant programs, which specifically target small,
local charities. This interview captures the essence of Satchel’s Pizza as not just a
restaurant, but as a vibrant community hub, a testament to resilience and artistic creativity.
Mr. Raye's reflections reveal how Satchel’s Pizza has become an enduring symbol of what
can be achieved when a business is built on principles of art, inclusivity, and genuine care
for its staff, customers and community.
