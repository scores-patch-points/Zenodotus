Human Rights Oral History Project
Interview with Roberto de Miranda and Soledad Rivas
Miami, Florida, April 23, 2013

Interview 5312.0010
Interviewed by Julio Estorino
Recorded by Javier Carrion
Outline by Julio Estorino and Nancy Cermeño
Interview with Roberto de Miranda and Soledad Rivas. Roberto de Miranda was a professor in
Cuba and the leader of El Colegio de Pedagogos Independientes (College of Independent
Teachers of Cuba). He also founded the Félix Varela Independent Library in 2000. In 2003, he
received the Pedro Luis Boitel Freedom Award. That same year, he was arrested during Cuba’s
Black Spring and sentenced to 20 years in prison. He was released after 14 months of jail
because of his health. Soledad Rivas, his wife, was a founding member of El Comité de Madres
(the Committee of Mothers), which grew into the Damas de Blanco (Ladies in White). While her
husband was in jail, she became an independent journalist reporting on the human rights
violations suffered by her family and other dissidents. She continued to protest with the Damas
de Blanco after her husband’s release. They both now live in exile in Miami.
This interview forms part of the Human Rights Oral History Project (HROHP) of the Cuban
Heritage Collection, University of Miami Libraries. The Project aims to capture the testimonies
of Cuban dissidents. In its first phase, HROHP focuses on the Black Spring of 2003 when the
Cuban government arrested 75 activists. Interviews were conducted with a number of the
dissidents who served time in prison as part of the Grupo de los 75 (Group of the 75) and with
members of the Damas de Blanco (Ladies in White), an organization of the wives, mothers,
daughters, and other female relatives of jailed dissidents. The Human Rights Oral History
Project was launched in 2013 with funding from the Marlins Foundation.
