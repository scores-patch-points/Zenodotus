Anthea Grant
Good afternoon. My name is Anthea Grant, and I am a Masters of Historic Preservation and
Conservation student. I'm here today in Charlotteville, Tobago and with me here, is a person who
used to “dance the cocoa”, and I'll let him introduce himself. So today is Sunday, July 2025. And
I'll pass over now to –
Anthony Marcano
Anthony Marcano
Anthea Grant
All right, Mr. Marcano, tell us a little bit about yourself and life in Toco, Trinidad, where you are
from and like life on the estate, dancing the cocoa. How was that experience for you?
Anthony Marcano
Well, it was a nice experience really, but at times it was really hard work. You really never see it
as really an enjoyment as a sport because we're working, so it come like a job, but dancing the
cocoa was really to get the gum off of the cocoa, so the cocoa used to sweat first and then you'll
put it in the cocoa box and you have to pull the cover and cover it like when rain fall, then you
cover it and then pull it back, and then after you come and mix up some lemon, lemon juice and
sprinkle it in the cocoa and then dance around, most of us will dance around, but it's a job, so.
Anthea Grant
But you used to make it fun.
Anthony Marcano
Yeah, it was fun really, but you’re working. But it was enjoyable, you know; it was really
enjoyable. And dancing, it was really, you know, a form of excitement, you know, but the hard
part of it is really when you're done; you had to clean out your foot and to get the gum off your
foot. That was a part of it. Yeah. A serious part of it really, you know, so. Yeah. It was a special
time. When you look back at it now, as I get older, I can realize it was just fun, because we don't

see no youths doing it these days that have that kind of fun, because cocoa becomes extinct in
Trinidad and Tobago as now. Nobody plants and has no set of farms going on for a long time. But
it was fun on the estate early.
Anthea Grant
Is there a special way to dance?
Anthony Marcano
No, but you have to dance in a way that you're pressing your foot on the cocoa itself to get the; to
feel it on your feet, you have to feel it on your feet.
Anthea Grant
Yeah.
Anthony Marcano
That you know you're naturally getting the gum off of the cocoa; it is not really just dancing, you
know, but you're just getting the gum off the cocoa. So, you know, and Anthea Grant
Is there a spread when you dance?
Anthony Marcano
Yeah, you would have, you would have who using the pal, they would take the pal and they would
bring it back under your foot but you have to dance around, you know, you're dancing in circles
and they try to bring it back center, so you keep dancing, going around, going around, and
sometimes you put your hand on one another shoulder and, you dancing and, it was nice, it was
nice, it was nice.
Anthea Grant
It's like a kind of Congo line.
Anthony Marcano
Yeah, it was, it was kind of, yeah. It was, it was, it was nice. It was nice.
Anthea Grant
And how do you think that, like, really brought the community together?
Anthony Marcano

It was, most of us were - it had a lot of older folks who were a part of that process really, but we
as some of the youngsters who mostly over the August holiday, took the opportunity to go through
that process, and it was nice.
Anthea Grant
So, what was the salary like?
Anthony Marcano
Well, the salary really; I used to really get $125 a fortnight.
Anthea Grant
Wow. What year was this?
Anthony Marcano
That was 1975, 76, 77. Oh, yeah, them’ years, ‘them’ years, yeah.
Anthea Grant
OK, great. Alright, well...could you give some last closing remarks on the importance of keeping
it alive?
Anthea Grant
Yeah, that is something I really would like to see. The CEPEP workers that they have right now, I
find they should take them and carry them in and spend on the cocoa a little more, the culture a
little more, the farming especially, bring back the cocoa trees and clean them up because it has a
lot of cocoa trees that's still being abandoned right now. Instead of going and cut grass all about
the place, you could go and clean the cocoa trees and bring them back because cocoa trees need
pruning —
Anthea Grant
For people who don't know what CEPEP is, because a lot of people would be outside of Trinidad,
explain what CEPEP is.
Anthony Marcano
CEPEP is a program that really, I should say, Patrick Manning really brought into play. It was to
really help all the ladies who were not financially supported, but it has become grass cutting right
now. That's where I have it, but they could take them back and go into the same cocoa fields, and
you don’t have to take the whole program, I mean, but some of the workers - carry them back into
the cocoa field and just clean the trees. And you'll get more cocoa if you clean the trees in them
and prune them, you know, take off all the vines and ‘thing’. Yeah, we have a lot of Government
estates in Tobago here. Have a lot of Government estates that need cleaning.

Anthea Grant
Yeah.
Anthony Marcano
And you could take some of the workers and carry them into the area and clean them, and you get
production. Yeah.
Anthea Grant
Actually, that is a really great suggestion. Well, thank you very much, Mr. Marcano, and do enjoy
the rest of your day.

Thank you very much
