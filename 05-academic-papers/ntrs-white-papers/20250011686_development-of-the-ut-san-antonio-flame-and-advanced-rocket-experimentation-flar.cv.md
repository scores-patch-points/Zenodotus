The FLame and Advanced Rocket Experimentation (FLARE) facility at The University of
Texas at San Antonio was built to close the design—build-test-iterate (DBTI) cycle for metal
additively manufactured (AM) propulsion hardware on a campus without existing test capability.
Hardware validation—a required step in DBTI-driven AM development—demands a controlled
hot fire test environment. FLARE was student-designed, student-sourced, and student-built to
establish a robust testbed for research-grade rocket and air-breathing combustor development
under tight resource constraints. The facility delivers up to 2 lb/s of stoichiometric gaseous
methane-oxygen to support ~500-Ibf thrust-class combustors, including rotating detonation
rocket engines (RDREs). The thrust stand structure sustains loads up to 1500 Ibf with a factor
of safety (FOS) of 4.
